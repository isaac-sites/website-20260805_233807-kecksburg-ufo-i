## Introduction

The Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) lawsuit over the 1965 Kecksburg incident demonstrated both the strengths and the limits of government transparency law. Journalist Leslie Kean successfully challenged NASA's initial handling of her records request, leading to a court-supervised search that uncovered hundreds of additional pages. However, the litigation could not reconstruct a complete historical record because FOIA is a disclosure statute, not an investigative or evidence-recovery mechanism. It can compel an agency to search for and release records that it possesses and controls, but it cannot recreate destroyed documents, compel one agency to produce another agency's files through a different agency, or prove that undocumented events occurred. These legal limits explain why the Kecksburg record remained incomplete even after years of litigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[govinfo.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">USCOURTS dcd 1 03 cv 02509 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case 1:03-cv-02509-EGS Document 45 Filed 03/27/07 Page 1 of 21August 12, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 12, 2025</span></span></span>

## What the court could require NASA to do

The most important achievement of the lawsuit was procedural rather than evidential. During the litigation, NASA acknowledged that its first two searches for responsive records had been inadequate. The federal court therefore required the agency to conduct a much broader search using additional offices, repositories and retired records rather than relying on the limited searches it had originally performed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">USCOURTS dcd 1 03 cv 02509 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case 1:03-cv-02509-EGS Document 45 Filed 03/27/07 Page 1 of 21August 12, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 12, 2025</span></span></span>

That distinction matters because FOIA obliges agencies to make a reasonable search for existing records. If a search is found inadequate, a court can require the agency to search again using appropriate methods. It cannot, however, assume that missing records still exist or order an agency to produce documents that it does not possess. FOIA's remedy is an improved search, not reconstruction of history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[govinfo.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">USCOURTS dcd 1 03 cv 02509 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case 1:03-cv-02509-EGS Document 45 Filed 03/27/07 Page 1 of 21August 12, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 12, 2025</span></span></span>

The expanded search ultimately produced hundreds of additional documents concerning NASA's handling of space-object recoveries and related historical activities. Even so, no document was found confirming that NASA had recovered an unusual craft from Kecksburg. The litigation therefore established that NASA's earlier search process had been deficient without establishing that responsive records had necessarily been concealed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">USCOURTS dcd 1 03 cv 02509 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case 1:03-cv-02509-EGS Document 45 Filed 03/27/07 Page 1 of 21August 12, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 12, 2025</span></span></span>

## Records outside NASA's possession

A central limitation of the case was that NASA could only be required to search [NASA records]({{ 'nasa-records/' | relative_url }}).

FOIA operates on an agency-by-agency basis. Each federal agency is responsible for searching the records under its own control. If potentially relevant material resides with another department—such as the Department of Defense, the Air Force or the State Department—a request directed to NASA cannot compel NASA to obtain and disclose those separate files. Likewise, an agency's ability to ask another organisation for records does not transform those records into NASA records for FOIA purposes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foia.gov/faq.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foia.gov">[foia.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FOI A.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Freedom of Information Act: Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)...</span></span></span>

For Kecksburg, this limitation was especially significant because claims about the incident have long involved multiple government organisations. Even if another agency once possessed operational reports, transport records or technical analyses, a NASA FOIA case could not compel their production unless they had become NASA records under NASA's control. As a result, even a successful lawsuit against NASA could leave substantial documentary gaps if relevant evidence lay elsewhere.

## Why missing or destroyed files could not be restored

FOIA assumes that agencies respond with records that already exist when the request is received. It does not require agencies to create new reports, reconstruct [missing files]({{ 'missing-files/' | relative_url }}), answer historical questions or recreate documents that were lawfully—or even mistakenly—disposed of decades earlier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foia.gov/how-to.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foia.gov">[foia.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FOI A.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Freedom of Information Act: How to Make a FOIA Request...</span></span></span>

This principle explains one of the recurring frustrations in the Kecksburg investigation. Researchers have pointed to references suggesting that some historical files were unavailable or no longer existed. Even if such records once contained useful information, a FOIA lawsuit could not compel NASA to recreate their contents from memory or secondary references. The legal obligation ended with conducting a reasonable search for surviving agency records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archives/oip/foia-guide-2004-edition-procedural-requirments" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">foia guide 2004 edition procedural requirments</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeOffice of Information Policy &#124; FOIA Guide, 2004 Edition: Procedural Requirments...</span></span></span>

Similarly, if records had been transferred, destroyed under records-management schedules or otherwise ceased to exist before the request, FOIA offered no mechanism for reconstructing their original contents. The absence of a file may be historically significant, but the statute provides no remedy capable of restoring lost evidence.

## Why witness claims cannot be proven by missing files

The Kecksburg incident illustrates a common misunderstanding about transparency law: the absence of documentary confirmation is not the same as documentary proof of a cover-up, but neither does it automatically disprove witness testimony.

FOIA can establish whether an agency conducted an adequate search and whether responsive records currently exist under its control. It cannot determine whether eyewitness recollections are accurate, whether undocumented actions occurred or whether an event was never recorded in surviving government files. Those questions require historical evaluation using multiple forms of evidence rather than public-records law alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">USCOURTS dcd 1 03 cv 02509 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case 1:03-cv-02509-EGS Document 45 Filed 03/27/07 Page 1 of 21August 12, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 12, 2025</span></span></span>

In the Kecksburg case, some observers interpreted missing files as evidence that records had been deliberately removed, while others argued that routine archival losses, dispersed recordkeeping and the passage of more than four decades offered more ordinary explanations. FOIA itself could not resolve that dispute because it is designed to provide access to existing agency records, not to adjudicate competing historical narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archives/oip/foia-guide-2004-edition-procedural-requirments" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">foia guide 2004 edition procedural requirments</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeOffice of Information Policy &#124; FOIA Guide, 2004 Edition: Procedural Requirments...</span></span></span>

## The broader transparency lesson

The Kecksburg litigation remains an instructive example of both the effectiveness and the limits of FOIA.

The lawsuit demonstrated that requesters can successfully challenge inadequate agency searches and require a more thorough review of historical holdings. That is an important accountability function, particularly where agencies initially fail to search all appropriate repositories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509/pdf/USCOURTS-dcd-1_03-cv-02509-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">USCOURTS dcd 1 03 cv 02509 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case 1:03-cv-02509-EGS Document 45 Filed 03/27/07 Page 1 of 21August 12, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 12, 2025</span></span></span>

At the same time, the case shows why even a successful FOIA action cannot necessarily reconstruct a decades-old historical event. If records have been lost, destroyed, never created, or remain in the custody of another agency, no court order under FOIA can fill those evidentiary gaps. For the Kecksburg incident, the result was greater transparency about NASA's archival practices and searches, but not a complete reconstruction of what happened in December 1965.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foia.gov/how-to.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foia.gov">[foia.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FOI A.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Freedom of Information Act: How to Make a FOIA Request...</span></span></span>
