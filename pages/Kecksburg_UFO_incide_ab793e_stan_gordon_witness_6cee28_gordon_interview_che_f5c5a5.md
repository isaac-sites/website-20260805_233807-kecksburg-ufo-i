## Introduction

A distinctive feature of [Stan Gordon]({{ 'stan-gordon/' | relative_url }})'s long-running investigation into the [Kecksburg UFO incident]({{ 'kecksburg-ufo-incident/' | relative_url }}) was his claim that he did not simply collect dramatic stories as they emerged. Instead, he said he tried to determine whether particular details had arisen independently from different witnesses rather than being repeated through rumour, media coverage or conversations within the community. In a case where memories accumulated over decades and the story became widely known, this approach was intended to reduce the risk of copied testimony. At the same time, because Gordon's interview process was largely documented through his own accounts rather than through contemporaneous interview records, outside researchers have found it difficult to evaluate exactly how effective these controls were.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stangordon.info/wp/news-events/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stangordon.info">[Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies Zone]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stangordon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneSighting Reports &amp; NEWS – Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneJuly 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2026</span></span></span>

## Withholding details during interviews

Gordon has repeatedly stated that he deliberately kept some reported details out of public discussion until he believed they had been confirmed by separate witnesses. His stated reasoning was straightforward: once a specific claim becomes widely known, later witnesses may consciously or unconsciously incorporate it into their own recollections.

According to Gordon, this meant that certain reported features of the Kecksburg case were not immediately published or discussed in lectures. Instead, he says he waited to see whether unrelated interviewees would mention them without prompting. Only after receiving what he regarded as sufficient independent [corroboration]({{ 'corroboration/' | relative_url }}) did he publicly describe some of those claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stangordon.info/wp/news-events/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stangordon.info">[Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies Zone]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stangordon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneSighting Reports &amp; NEWS – Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneJuly 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2026</span></span></span>

One example he has highlighted concerns reports that **two military flatbed tractor-trailers**, rather than one, were present during the alleged recovery operation. Gordon has written that he intentionally withheld this claim for years until multiple witnesses—including, according to his account, one of the truck drivers—provided similar information independently. He presented this as an example of his broader interviewing strategy rather than as proof that the underlying event necessarily occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stangordon.info/wp/news-events/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stangordon.info">[Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies Zone]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stangordon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneSighting Reports &amp; NEWS – Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneJuly 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2026</span></span></span>

This method reflects a common investigative principle in witness interviewing: information volunteered without prompting generally carries more evidential value than details supplied after exposure to other accounts. However, Gordon's implementation relied primarily on his own notes and recollections rather than on a published interview protocol or independently archived interview transcripts.

## Revisiting locations with witnesses

Gordon also emphasised returning to original locations with witnesses whenever possible instead of relying solely on telephone conversations or later written statements.

Site visits served several purposes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* allowing witnesses to indicate precisely where they had been standing;
* checking whether claimed sightlines were physically plausible;
* comparing different witnesses' positions without necessarily introducing them to one another;
* clarifying distances, terrain and landmarks that might otherwise become confused over time.

</div>

Gordon has described conducting both face-to-face interviews and repeated follow-up conversations over many years, particularly with witnesses who had remained reluctant to speak publicly. Revisiting locations also enabled him to compare descriptions against the geography of the woods, nearby roads and surrounding farmland instead of relying entirely on memory detached from place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stangordon.info/wp/news-events/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stangordon.info">[Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies Zone]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stangordon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneSighting Reports &amp; NEWS – Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneJuly 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2026</span></span></span>

This approach is consistent with established oral-history practice, where returning participants to meaningful locations can sometimes help recover contextual details. At the same time, environmental cues can also reinforce later reconstructions of memory, making the effect difficult to interpret decades after an event.

## Looking for converging details rather than identical stories

Gordon has often argued that he was less interested in identical narratives than in recurring details emerging from people who occupied different positions during the incident.

Rather than expecting every witness to describe the same sequence of events, he looked for overlap in specific elements, such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* reports of military roadblocks;
* descriptions of unusual military vehicles;
* the timing of vehicle movements;
* the object's reported shape and seamless appearance;
* claims that military personnel restricted civilian access.

</div>

In Gordon's view, convergence among witnesses who did not appear to know one another strengthened confidence that at least some portions of the broader narrative reflected genuine observations rather than later storytelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stangordon.info/wp/news-events/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stangordon.info">[Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies Zone]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stangordon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneSighting Reports &amp; NEWS – Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneJuly 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2026</span></span></span>

Critics, however, note that the Kecksburg incident became one of the best-known American UFO cases. By the 1970s and especially after national television coverage in 1990, many commonly repeated features of the story were already circulating publicly. Consequently, later agreement between witnesses does not automatically demonstrate independent memory.

## Why independent memories remained difficult to establish

The principal challenge facing Gordon's method was the passage of time.

Many of the interviews that became central to the modern Kecksburg narrative occurred years—or in some cases decades—after December 1965. During that period witnesses could have encountered newspaper articles, documentaries, public lectures, conversations with neighbours or previous investigators. Even if individuals sincerely believed they were recalling original memories, psychological research shows that long-term recollection can gradually absorb information acquired after the original event.

The Kecksburg community itself presented an additional complication. Because the alleged crash became an enduring local story, many residents already shared a common framework before formal interviews took place. Distinguishing genuinely independent recollection from community narrative therefore became increasingly difficult over time.

## Why undocumented controls remain hard to evaluate

Gordon's interviewing approach is frequently cited by supporters as evidence that he attempted to minimise contamination between witnesses rather than simply collecting sensational accounts. His practice of withholding selected information until separate witnesses mentioned it voluntarily is one of the most distinctive methodological claims associated with his investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stangordon.info/wp/news-events/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stangordon.info">[Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies Zone]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stangordon.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneSighting Reports &amp; NEWS – Stan Gordon&#x27;s UFO Anomalies ZoneJuly 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2026</span></span></span>

However, historians and other researchers face important limitations when assessing that claim. Most interviews were conducted privately, and comprehensive interview logs, timestamps, questionnaires, recordings and version histories have not been published in a way that allows independent reconstruction of exactly what each witness knew before being interviewed. Without such documentation, outside observers cannot fully verify which details were genuinely elicited independently, which emerged after repeated interviews, or how questioning evolved over time.

As a result, Gordon's methods occupy an intermediate position. They demonstrate a conscious effort to guard against copied testimony in a case shaped by decades of [retelling]({{ 'retelling/' | relative_url }}), yet the absence of a complete, independently auditable interview archive means the effectiveness of those safeguards cannot itself be tested with the same degree of confidence that Gordon sought from his witnesses.
