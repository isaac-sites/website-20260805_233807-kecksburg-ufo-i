## Introduction

One of the most influential early arguments for the Kecksburg UFO story was Ivan T. Sanderson's claim that the object followed a controlled flight path from Lapeer, Michigan, through northern Ohio and ultimately to Kecksburg, Pennsylvania. His reconstruction appeared to show a slow-moving object that altered course in flight, apparently ruling out an ordinary meteor.

Subsequent examination of the underlying reports, however, found that the route depended on combining unrelated "landing" claims, treating approximate witness times as if they were precise measurements, and introducing a significant calculation error. Rather than revealing a continuous flight path, Sanderson's map illustrates how scattered local reports from a widely visible fireball were transformed into a single narrative that later writers repeated for decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/SUNlite3_6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo Shedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UfoShedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOsDecember 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2025</span></span></span>

## How separate reports became a single route

Sanderson published his reconstruction within days of the 9 December 1965 fireball. Instead of beginning with astronomical observations or measured trajectories, he connected locations where people believed debris had fallen or the object had descended. His principal chain linked:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* Lapeer, Michigan
* Lorain County (near Elyria), Ohio
* Midland, Pennsylvania
* Kecksburg, Pennsylvania

</div>

He argued that joining these locations "automatically" revealed the object's path and demonstrated that it had turned east before descending into western Pennsylvania. This became one of the earliest foundations for claims that the Kecksburg object was not behaving like a meteor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/SUNlite3_6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo Shedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UfoShedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOsDecember 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2025</span></span></span>

The weakness of this approach is that none of these reports represented established navigation points. They were independent local interpretations made by observers spread across hundreds of kilometres during an exceptionally bright bolide. People in many communities sincerely believed the object had come down nearby, creating numerous mutually incompatible "landing sites" across Michigan, Ohio and Pennsylvania. Later reviews have identified many more reported impact locations than Sanderson incorporated into his route, making his selection highly subjective rather than comprehensive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/SUNlite3_6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo Shedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UfoShedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOsDecember 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2025</span></span></span>

The individual reports themselves also proved unreliable as markers of the object's passage. The metallic strips found near Lapeer were investigated and identified as radar chaff rather than fragments from the fireball. Likewise, no verified debris was recovered from Midland, while reports from Lorain County were never established as material from the atmospheric event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/SUNlite3_6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo Shedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UfoShedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOsDecember 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2025</span></span></span>

## The miles-per-minute calculation mistake

An equally important problem concerns Sanderson's speed estimate.

He argued that the reconstructed route implied an object travelling at only around 1,000 miles per hour (roughly 1,600 km/h), dramatically slower than a meteor and therefore suggestive of controlled flight. This estimate became one of the most frequently repeated numerical arguments in later UFO literature.

Later investigators found that the published calculations confused **miles per minute** with **miles per hour**. The arithmetic itself corresponded to distance travelled each minute, but the units were presented as hourly speed, reducing the reported velocity by a factor of sixty. Critics have described this as a fundamental unit-conversion error rather than evidence of an unusually slow craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/SUNlite3_6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo Shedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UfoShedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOsDecember 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2025</span></span></span>

Even before considering uncertainties in witness timing, this mistake undermines the headline claim that the reconstructed path demonstrated impossibly slow motion.

The timing data themselves also lacked the precision needed for such calculations. Witnesses generally estimated events from memory, often without synchronised clocks, while newspapers frequently rounded times or reported them approximately. Combining these estimates into a minute-by-minute track gave an appearance of precision that the original observations did not possess.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/SUNlite3_6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo Shedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UfoShedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOsDecember 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why the plotted route fails geographically

Independent examination of the geometry exposed further problems.

Sanderson argued that the object executed roughly a 25-degree turn before reaching Pennsylvania. When later researchers plotted the same locations on accurate maps instead of a hand-drawn sketch, the geometry changed substantially. The line from Lapeer to Elyria lies at approximately 152 degrees, while the line from Elyria to Kecksburg lies near 120 degrees—a difference of about 32 degrees rather than 25. More importantly, extending the plotted route does not actually pass through Kecksburg. Instead, it misses the town by roughly 26 miles (42 km) to the south.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/SUNlite3_6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo Shedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UfoShedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOsDecember 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2025</span></span></span>

That discrepancy is significant because Kecksburg is the very destination the route was intended to explain. If the geometry does not intersect the reported crash location, the claimed reconstructed flight path cannot serve as evidence that a single object navigated to Kecksburg.

The route also omitted other contemporary reports that would have complicated the reconstruction. For example, witnesses near Erie, Pennsylvania, reported the fireball disappearing over Lake Erie rather than continuing towards Kecksburg. Air Force investigators even examined reports from that region, yet these observations were excluded from Sanderson's plotted sequence. Selecting only those reports that supported one desired [trajectory]({{ 'trajectory/' | relative_url }}) created a classic example of confirmation bias rather than an objective reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/SUNlite3_6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo Shedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UfoShedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOsDecember 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2025</span></span></span>

## What the false route demonstrates

The Lapeer-to-Kecksburg route remains historically important because it shows how a persuasive narrative can emerge from individually sincere but incompatible reports.

Three factors combined to produce the illusion of a coherent flight path:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Selective linking of reports.** Only certain claimed landing locations were connected, while others were ignored.
* **Over-precise timing.** Approximate witness recollections were treated as though they formed a continuous chronological track.
* **Faulty speed calculations.** A units error greatly understated the implied velocity, making ordinary atmospheric flight appear impossible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/SUNlite3_6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo Shedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UfoShedding some light on UFOlogy and UFOsDecember 4, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2025</span></span></span>

</div>

Rather than demonstrating that an object slowly manoeuvred from Michigan to Pennsylvania, later analyses conclude that Sanderson's reconstruction illustrates the risks of combining scattered eyewitness reports without independently verifying either their timing or their geographical relationship.
