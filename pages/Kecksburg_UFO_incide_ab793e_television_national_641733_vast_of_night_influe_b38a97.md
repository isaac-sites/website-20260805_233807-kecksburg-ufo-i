## Introduction

*The Vast of Night* (2019) is not a film adaptation of the [Kecksburg UFO incident]({{ 'kecksburg-ufo-incident/' | relative_url }}), but director Andrew Patterson has explicitly identified Kecksburg as one of its principal inspirations. Rather than recreating the 1965 Pennsylvania event, the film borrows its storytelling mechanics: a small-town radio station becomes the first place where unusual reports converge, ordinary citizens are left to piece together fragmentary evidence, and official institutions remain distant, opaque and potentially untrustworthy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2020/05/vast-of-night-true-story-amazon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vanityfair.com">[Vanity Fair]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vanityfair.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vanity Fair The Weird, True Stories That Inspired The Vast of Night</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The film follows a 1950s New Mexico switchboard operator and a radio DJ as they investigate mysterious signals and lights. That approach illustrates how the Kecksburg legend evolved beyond UFO investigation into modern science fiction. Instead of focusing on whether an unidentified object really crashed, *The Vast of Night* captures the atmosphere that made Kecksburg culturally enduring—local witnesses hearing strange accounts before the wider public, rumours spreading through broadcast media, and uncertainty proving more compelling than definitive answers.

## The Kecksburg elements cited by the director

Andrew Patterson has been unusually direct about Kecksburg's influence. He explained that much of the plot surrounding radio disc jockey Everett Sloan grew from the Kecksburg story, particularly reports that local broadcaster John Murphy at radio station WHJB received calls after the incident and later believed government officials had confiscated recordings connected to his reporting. Patterson described this sequence—not the alleged spacecraft itself—as one of the narrative foundations for his film.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2020/05/vast-of-night-true-story-amazon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vanityfair.com">[Vanity Fair]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vanityfair.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vanity Fair The Weird, True Stories That Inspired The Vast of Night</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The film follows a 1950s New Mexico switchboard operator and a radio DJ as they investigate mysterious signals and lights. One key inspir...</span></span></span>

The result is a noticeable shift in emphasis. Whereas many earlier fictional works inspired by UFO cases concentrate on recovered craft or extraterrestrial beings, *The Vast of Night* centres on communication:

* telephone operators connecting frightened callers;
* a local radio presenter gathering testimony in real time;
* mysterious audio signals interrupting ordinary broadcasts;
* incomplete information spreading faster than official explanations.

These elements mirror the communications dimension of Kecksburg rather than attempting to dramatise the incident as a historical reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2020/05/vast-of-night-true-story-amazon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vanityfair.com">[Vanity Fair]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vanityfair.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vanity Fair The Weird, True Stories That Inspired The Vast of Night</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The film follows a 1950s New Mexico switchboard operator and a radio DJ as they investigate mysterious signals and lights. Another important difference is chronology. Kecksburg occurred in 1965, while *The Vast of Night* is set during the 1950s. Patterson deliberately relocated the story into an earlier Cold War setting, allowing him to evoke the cultural mood surrounding UFO reports without depicting the historical incident itself. The film therefore functions as an original period mystery shaped by the emotional logic of Kecksburg instead of its documented timeline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vast_of_Night" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Vast of Night</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Vast of Night</span></span></span>

## Radio voices and inaccessible authorities

One of the strongest echoes of Kecksburg is the way information flows through local media before any official response appears.

During the real Kecksburg incident, much of the earliest public understanding developed through eyewitness conversations, local reporting and radio communication. Later accounts surrounding John Murphy became an enduring part of the legend, reinforcing the idea that broadcasters occupied a unique position between witnesses and government authorities. Whether every aspect of those later stories can be verified remains disputed, but they became an influential part of the mythology that Patterson drew upon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2020/05/vast-of-night-true-story-amazon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vanityfair.com">[Vanity Fair]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vanityfair.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vanity Fair The Weird, True Stories That Inspired The Vast of Night</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The film follows a 1950s New Mexico switchboard operator and a radio DJ as they investigate mysterious signals and lights. One key inspir...</span></span></span>

In *The Vast of Night*, Everett's radio programme performs a similar narrative function. Rather than serving as a conventional news bulletin, the station becomes a gathering point where isolated experiences begin forming a larger pattern. The audience hears fragmented testimony, uncertain memories and unexplained sounds before any institutional explanation arrives.

This mirrors a defining characteristic of the Kecksburg legend: local voices possess immediate experiences, while official organisations remain largely off-screen. The resulting imbalance creates tension not because viewers are shown a conspiracy, but because they experience uncertainty from the perspective of ordinary residents.

The film reinforces this perspective by keeping military and governmental actors largely invisible. Their absence is dramatically significant. Instead of confronting visible antagonists, the protagonists struggle against incomplete information, inaccessible authority and the possibility that important events are occurring beyond public view. That dynamic closely resembles the narrative structure that has sustained public interest in Kecksburg for decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vast_of_Night" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Vast of Night</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Vast of Night</span></span></span>
